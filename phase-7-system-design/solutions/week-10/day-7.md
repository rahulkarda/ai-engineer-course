# Solutions — Week 10 · Day 7 (Sunday): Reflection & Self-Assessment Notes

## Reflection guidance

Day 7 reflection prompts have no single correct answer. The following notes provide calibration guidance rather than model answers.

## Competency confidence map — calibration guidance

**Rating 1–2 (weak):** You either have no story, or your best story is more than 5 years old and lacks a quantified result. This is a development area — note it and actively seek opportunities to build this competency over the next 6–12 months.

**Rating 3 (adequate):** You have a story, it follows STAR, but the Result is thin (no strong metric, or the outcome was modest). This will answer the question but won't differentiate you. Worth strengthening before a senior/staff interview.

**Rating 4–5 (strong):** Recent story, personal ownership, quantified result, genuine learning. This competency is interview-ready.

**The most common pattern:** Engineers tend to rate themselves 4–5 on Impact (they remember projects they shipped) and 1–2 on Failure and Conflict (they find those uncomfortable to discuss). If this is your pattern, spend preparation time specifically on the Failure and Conflict stories — they are the highest-signal questions for senior roles precisely because most candidates avoid them.

---

## Story bank gap analysis — guidance

If a competency has no P-coverage after 8 stories, consider:

1. **Reframe an existing story.** Is there a story you rated as Secondary for that competency that could be made Primary with a different opening and emphasis?

2. **Expand the time window.** Is there a story from 3–6 years ago that would serve? Note the age honestly ("this was a few years ago, but it's the clearest example I have of...").

3. **Accept the gap and fill it in real life.** If you genuinely have no mentoring story, the answer is not to invent one — it is to seek a mentoring opportunity in the next quarter and document it as it happens.

## Design doc reflection — guidance

**"Hardest section to write" signal table:**

| Hardest section | What it might reveal |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Problem | You haven't fully validated that this is the right problem to solve |
| Non-goals | Scope hasn't been decided — there are stakeholders who haven't agreed on the boundary |
| Alternatives | You came to this design without genuinely evaluating other approaches |
| Open questions | Key stakeholders haven't been consulted yet |
| Success metrics | The project doesn't have a clear definition of done |

The section that is hardest to write is usually the most important section. If writing the Non-goals section revealed that two teams have conflicting expectations about scope, that conflict needs to be resolved before implementation begins — the design doc did its job.
